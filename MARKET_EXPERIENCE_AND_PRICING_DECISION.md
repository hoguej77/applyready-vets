# ApplyReady Vets market, experience, and pricing decision

Last researched: July 30, 2026

## Decision

ApplyReady Vets will compete as a **veteran application workspace**, not as another AI template gallery.

The consumer offer is:

- Free download.
- One complete, exportable, tracked application workspace free.
- **ApplyReady Vets Complete: $5.99 once** in the U.S. storefront for unlimited tracked application workspaces.
- No subscription, ads, AI-credit packs, feature bundles, or export ransom.
- No consumer tip ladder; Complete is the only consumer purchase.
- Safety, privacy, source review, data deletion, and access to work a person already created are never paywalled.

This replaces the earlier $24.99 decision and the still earlier founding/mature-price ladder. At $5.99, Complete costs
roughly one week in several audited subscription competitors while keeping the app's promise unusually simple.

## Market evidence

### What earns strong reviews

- A guided flow that removes blank-page anxiety.
- Automatic, professional formatting.
- Helpful examples and editable suggestions.
- Fast creation and a clear finished artifact.
- Multiple resume versions.
- Reliable save, preview, PDF export, email, and Files handoff.
- A genuinely usable free experience.

### What drives low reviews

- A paywall appearing only after the user has entered an entire resume.
- Weekly auto-renewing prices and confusing product ladders.
- Paid plans that still impose hidden AI-use limits.
- Separate mobile and web purchases.
- Lost drafts, missing older resumes, sign-in loops, crashes, and frozen export screens.
- AI that changes the candidate's meaning or produces generic filler.
- Rigid templates, missing fields, and date/format controls that do not work.
- Vague “ATS scores,” claims that contact information was dropped, and no explanation of what was actually checked.
- No clear way to recover work, contact support, or understand data handling.

### What competitor screenshots overemphasize

The leading screenshot sets are dominated by template grids, document previews, bright marketing colors, “AI”
callouts, and broad “job-winning” or “ATS” language. Those patterns communicate output quickly, but they leave little
room for evidence control, the larger application process, federal requirements, service translation, deadlines,
version history, or the emotional reality of leaving military service.

## The gap ApplyReady Vets should own

The market has many resume generators. It does not have a mature, local-first iPhone workspace that combines:

1. A real job posting as the organizing object.
2. Military-to-civilian translation backed by official O*NET data.
3. Separate evidence, content, federal, and formatting readiness checks.
4. Candidate approval of every proposed statement through Truth Lock.
5. Civilian and federal workflows in one product.
6. LinkedIn and job-site handoffs without credential collection, scraping, fake integration, or auto-apply.
7. Application status, deadline, notes, required documents, and packet-version history.
8. Protected autosave, recovery, deterministic local processing, and explicit cloud consent.
9. Dated official sources and a clear non-affiliation statement.
10. A transparent lifetime price.

The brand promise is therefore:

> Build the application—not just the resume.

## Experience architecture

Apple's current guidance says tabs should represent stable top-level destinations, onboarding should be fast and
optional, and instruction should appear close to the action. VA's veteran-centered guidance emphasizes plain language,
mobile-first prioritization, accessibility, no dead ends, and one useful next step.

Sources:

- [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)
- [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Search fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/search-fields)
- [VA.gov design principles](https://design.va.gov/about/principles)
- [VA.gov content principles](https://design.va.gov/content-style-guide/content-principles)
- [VA.gov accessibility guidance](https://design.va.gov/accessibility/)

The implemented top-level structure is:

| Section | Job to be done |
| --- | --- |
| **Today** | Show one next action, pipeline health, recent applications, and a direct first-application action. |
| **Workspace** | Import, profile, analyze, review through Truth Lock, and export one application packet. |
| **Applications** | Search and manage jobs, statuses, deadlines, notes, required documents, and versions. |
| **Toolkit** | Find the military crosswalk, civilian/federal modes, LinkedIn kit, official sources, and privacy help. |

Design rules:

- Keep the logo on the welcome surface and out of the daily work hierarchy.
- Use system typography, SF Symbols, native navigation, Dynamic Type, and semantic system colors.
- Use one strong action per section and compact rows for supporting actions.
- Never use color alone to communicate status.
- Show an understandable empty state with a real next action.
- Use “AI” only where the processing method matters; do not make it the personality of the product.
- Never show a single mysterious ATS score.
- Keep legal and non-affiliation language available without forcing the user through a wall of disclaimers.

## $200,000 operating-profit path

This is a planning model, not a guarantee. “Profit” below means target operating contribution before the owner's
personal income tax. Refunds, sales taxes, Apple adjustments, actual support costs, and acquisition costs can change it.

Assuming eligibility and enrollment in Apple's Small Business Program:

| Input | Planning value |
| --- | ---: |
| Verified installs | 100,000 |
| Lifetime conversion | 47.14% |
| Complete purchases | 47,140 |
| Price | $5.99 once |
| Customer spend | $282,369 |
| Apple commission at 15% | $42,355 |
| Estimated developer proceeds before other costs | $240,014 |
| Operating, legal, support, research, and marketing reserve | $40,000 |
| Target operating contribution | **$200,000** |

[Apple's Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/) currently states a
15% commission on paid apps and In-App Purchases for qualifying developers up to the program threshold. Enrollment is
not automatic and must be completed by the Account Holder.

The model needs about 47,140 lifetime purchases to retain a $40,000 operating reserve and reach $200,000 of contribution.
That would be roughly 47% conversion at 100,000 installs—too aggressive for a base case. The $5.99 consumer price should
therefore optimize trust, reach, and review growth; organization sponsorship and licensing must carry a material share
of the revenue and recurring hosted-service costs.

## Organization revenue without consumer upsells

After the consumer product demonstrates completion, retention, and accessibility:

- Run three to five paid pilots with colleges, workforce boards, transition programs, and veteran-serving nonprofits.
- Price early pilots by cohort and support burden, not by hiding features from individual veterans.
- Include sponsored Complete access, staff onboarding, privacy-preserving outcome reporting, and a named support path.
- Do not sell personal resume content or individual-level veteran activity to an employer.
- Keep hosted AI and support costs inside the organization contract instead of creating a consumer credit ladder.

## Metrics that decide whether the plan works

Downloads alone do not prove product-market fit.

| Metric | Initial target |
| --- | ---: |
| Welcome to first imported job or resume | 50%+ |
| First import to completed analysis | 45%+ |
| Analysis to reviewed export | 35%+ |
| Four-week return | 20%+ |
| Lifetime conversion after one free workspace | 10%–12% |
| Crash-free sessions | 99.8%+ |
| Successful export attempts | 99.5%+ |
| Critical unsupported claims escaping Truth Lock | 0 |
| VoiceOver task completion in moderated testing | 90%+ |

The primary product KPI is **completed trusted application packets**, not “AI generations,” time spent, or a single
ATS number.
